RebelBetting vs BetBurger

BetBurger advertises "600+ bookmakers". RebelBetting advertises "100+". Read side by side that looks like a six-to-one difference. It isn't — the two numbers count different things, and once you put them in the same unit the gap is roughly a quarter.

Affiliate disclosure: both products are approved affiliate partners of this site. The bookmaker figures below were counted by us, not copied from either vendor's marketing. BetBurger's are tested — read on 2026-07-31 from inside a paid account the vendor provided for review. RebelBetting's are observed — taken the same day from the public bookmaker list at rebelbetting.com/bookmakers. We have not yet been inside RebelBetting's product, and we say so rather than implying otherwise.

The headline numbers are not the same unit

Both products carry the same underlying concept: a clone is a mirror or regional domain running the same book's odds under a different brand. In BetBurger's list, the 1xbet entry alone carries sixteen. Both vendors track clones, and RebelBetting even publishes a guide encouraging their use. Only one of the two puts them in the headline count.

Anyone choosing between these two on the strength of "600+ versus 100+" is making a decision on a number that does not mean what it appears to mean. To be fair to BetBurger: the real breakdown is disclosed inside the product, on the same screen we read it from. It is the marketing page, not the software, that creates the impression.

A point to RebelBetting on transparency

RebelBetting publishes a live status for every bookmaker on its list, and currently marks fourteen as disconnected — Bethard, Eurobet, NedsAU, Novibet, Palmerbet, PlayUpAU, Sisal, Skybet, Snai, StarCasino, TitanBet, BetDEX, Betway and NEObet. Publishing your own outages is not a comfortable thing for a vendor to do, and it lets a buyer check whether the books they actually hold are working before paying. We found no equivalent in BetBurger's account interface. That is a real and checkable difference in favour of RebelBetting, and it runs opposite to the commission incentive on this page.

What we still cannot tell you

Coverage is not the same as opportunity. What neither of us has measured yet is how many usable arbs each product actually surfaces across a normal week for the books you can hold, and whether those opportunities survive long enough to place at the odds shown. That is the number that decides whether a subscription pays for itself, and it needs funded accounts on both sides. We would rather leave it blank than guess at it. Two specific gaps: we have not been inside RebelBetting's product, so its figures here are read from a public page; and RebelBetting's published list is served from its value-betting product, so we cannot yet confirm the sure-betting list is identical.

Provisional verdict

RebelBetting is the more guided workflow and the more transparent one about its own failures. BetBurger has the genuinely deeper feed and the prematch/live split that higher-volume users want, and its interface timestamps every opportunity, which matters once you care about whether an arb is still alive. On price, BetBurger's observed 30-day Prematch plan and RebelBetting's annual Starter tier are close enough that coverage fit, not cost, should decide it.

Neither gets a score from us yet. Ranking two products on feature lists before testing whether either one makes money is how most of this category's reviews are written, and it is not useful to you.
